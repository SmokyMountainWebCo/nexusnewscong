# Tropical Storm Simon weakens off Mexico's Baja California coast

Tropical Storm Simon is weakening over open water south of Mexico's Baja California peninsula, and forecasters expect it to fall apart this weekend, the U.S. National Hurricane Center said Friday.

As of Advisory No. 37, issued at 15:00 GMT on Oct. 16, Simon had maximum sustained winds of 40 mph, or 65 kph. Its center sat about 160 miles, or 260 kilometers, south of Cabo San Lucas, and it was moving northwest roughly parallel to Baja California Sur.

"There are no coastal watches or warnings in effect," the hurricane center said.

The center said wind shear and dry air are weakening the storm. "Southwesterly vertical wind shear and dry mid-level air are taking a toll on Simon this morning," forecasters wrote. They expect Simon to become a post-tropical remnant low on Saturday and to dissipate within 48 hours.

The storm's main threat to land comes from swells. The hurricane center said the swells "are likely to cause life-threatening surf and rip current conditions" along parts of west-central Mexico and Baja California Sur.

The Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System, or GDACS, still lists an overall Orange alert for Simon. That rating appears to reflect the storm's stronger phase earlier in its life, which began Oct. 7. GDACS rates the current episode Green, its lowest level. A GDACS model estimates that about 62 people fall within the storm's Category 1 wind zone, but no land warnings are in effect.

Earlier this week, Simon drew attention as a hurricane. India Today reported on Oct. 14 that a satellite captured Hurricane Simon creating gravity waves.

No damage or casualties had been reported in the sources reviewed for this report.
