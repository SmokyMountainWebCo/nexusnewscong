# Fact check: Nov. 17 is a one-minute alert test, not the start of a 90-day blackout

PolitiFact rated the claim that a 90-day power, internet or banking blackout begins Nov. 17 "False." Snopes found no evidence for it, and France 24 called it a hoax. What is scheduled is a routine nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System by FEMA and the Federal Communications Commission.

The test is set for 2:20 p.m. EST Tuesday, according to an FCC public notice (DA 26-989) issued Sept. 16. FEMA said the test is "expected to last about one minute" on TV and radio, and that Wireless Emergency Alerts will not be part of this year's test, so phones will not sound. NOAA Weather Radios will not receive it either, the National Weather Service said. The FCC notice gives the message text: "This is only a test. No action is required by the public." If weather or a real emergency interferes, the backup date is Dec. 3. No official announcement of any power, internet or banking shutdown exists.

Snopes said the rumor felt "unusually plausible" because it followed Microsoft's outage, the FAA's nationwide ground stop and bank software problems on Nov. 16. Officials attributed each of those to technical causes, not cyberattacks. Snopes said it would not rate the future-tense prediction until Nov. 17 has passed.

France 24 reported that the rumor grew out of the routine test and was amplified by cryptocurrency accounts urging people to withdraw their savings. It said Russian-language accounts tied the claim to genuine European Union advice that households keep 72 hours of supplies. France 24 did not report a formal government or platform attribution of an influence operation.

Posts also cited a 2023 episode of "The Simpsons" with a Nov. 17 blackout plot. Both outlets said the episode does not depict a global cyberattack.
