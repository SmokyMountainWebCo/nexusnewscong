# Satellite monitors flag low-impact forest fires in Botswana and Mozambique

A global disaster-alert system recorded three forest fires in southern Africa this month: one in northern Botswana and two in central Mozambique. The system rated all three at its lowest alert level.

The Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System, or GDACS, rated each fire "Green," its lowest alert level. That rating signals limited expected humanitarian impact.

In Botswana, a fire near the Chobe area burned from Oct. 12 to Oct. 13, according to GDACS event data. The system's latest figure puts the burned area at 7,856 hectares, about 19,400 acres. An earlier GDACS report page listed 6,889 hectares and said "People affected: 0 in the burned area." The same page said the fire "can have a low humanitarian impact based on the burned area and the affected population and their vulnerability."

In Mozambique, about 1,200 kilometers to the northeast, GDACS logged two fires in the Zambezia and Nampula area. One burned from Oct. 6 to Oct. 14 and covered 11,196 hectares. The other burned from Oct. 6 to Oct. 13 and covered 5,655 hectares. GDACS rated both fires Green and reported no humanitarian impact for either.

No source reviewed for this report identified what caused any of the three fires. Nexus News Media found no coverage of these specific events from Botswana's government, ReliefWeb, Reuters, The Associated Press or the Botswana newspaper Mmegi.

The fires fall within the region's usual burning season. A research preprint posted on EGUsphere, which has not been peer reviewed, says fires in the region "typically begin in May and intensify markedly from August through October as vegetation dries, fuel loads accumulate, and hot, dry, and windy conditions prevail."

GDACS builds its fire alerts from satellite detections and estimates impact from burned area and nearby population. Its figures can change as new detections arrive, which may explain the gap between the two Botswana burned-area numbers.

Nexus News Media will update this report if officials release information on causes or damage.
