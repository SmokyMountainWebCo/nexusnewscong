# Satellite monitors flag low-impact forest fires in Botswana and Mozambique

*Updated Oct. 16, 2026: Adds a larger fire in Mozambique's Zambezia province and GDACS counts of people living in the burned areas.*

A global disaster-alert system has recorded four forest fires in southern Africa this month: one in northern Botswana and three in central Mozambique. The system rated all four at its lowest alert level.

The Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System, or GDACS, rated each fire "Green," its lowest alert level. GDACS says such fires "can have a low humanitarian impact based on the burned area and the affected population and their vulnerability."

In Botswana, a fire in the Chobe District burned from Oct. 12 to Oct. 13, according to GDACS event data. The system's latest figure puts the burned area at 7,856 hectares, about 19,400 acres. GDACS listed zero people living in the burned area.

In Mozambique, about 1,250 kilometers to the northeast, GDACS logged three fires in Zambezia province. The largest burned about 32,246 hectares, roughly 79,700 acres, from Oct. 5 to Oct. 16. A second burned 11,196 hectares from Oct. 6 to Oct. 14, and GDACS counted 599 people living in its burned area. A third burned 5,655 hectares from Oct. 6 to Oct. 13, with 1,754 people in its burned area. GDACS did not report casualties or damage for any of the fires.

No source reviewed for this report identified what caused any of the fires, and no source links the Mozambique fires to the one in Botswana. Nexus News Media found no news coverage of these specific events in Botswanan or Mozambican media, and the GDACS media feeds for three of the fires returned no items.

The Botswana fire falls within that country's usual burning season. A research preprint posted on EGUsphere, which has not been peer reviewed, says Botswana fires "typically begin in May and intensify markedly from August through October as vegetation dries, fuel loads accumulate, and hot, dry, and windy conditions prevail." Botswana's government-run Daily News has said the country's fire season runs from May to November.

GDACS builds its fire alerts from satellite detections and estimates impact from burned area and nearby population. Its figures can change as new detections arrive. An earlier GDACS report page listed the Botswana fire at 6,889 hectares, which may reflect an earlier detection.

Nexus News Media will update this report if officials release information on causes or damage.
