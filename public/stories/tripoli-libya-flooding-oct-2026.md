# Flash flooding in Tripoli kills at least one, prompts investigation

Torrential rain flooded Libya's capital, Tripoli, on Tuesday, killing at least one person, the Libya Herald reported. Some reports put the death toll at two.

The city received about 152 millimeters of rain in a single day, according to the Libya Herald. The paper said that compares with an annual national average of 200 to 400 millimeters and an October average of 25 to 42 millimeters.

"At least one person drowned," the Libya Herald reported. A separate report carried by the WN.com news aggregator, whose original outlet could not be identified, said the flooding killed "at least two people," citing authorities and local media. That report said the Libyan Red Crescent deployed emergency teams as downpours flooded neighborhoods and damaged buildings and vehicles.

Issam al-Tamouni, housing minister in the Tripoli-based government, announced a technical investigation into the flooding of city roads.

"What has come to light is a painful reality and proof that some roads did not meet the required standards," al-Tamouni said, according to the Libya Herald. "We will launch a technical investigation, and we will not cover up its findings."

He said the government would begin paying the first installment of compensation next week, the paper reported in its Oct. 15 edition.

The Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System, or GDACS, logged the flood in Tripoli from Oct. 13 to Oct. 15 and rated it Green, its lowest alert level, based on data from the Global Flood Awareness System.

Nexus News Media found no statement on the flooding from Libya's national authorities and has not independently confirmed the casualty figures with the Libyan Red Crescent or the U.N. humanitarian office.

Nexus News Media will update this report as officials release casualty and damage figures.
