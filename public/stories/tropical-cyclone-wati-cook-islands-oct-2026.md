# Tropical Cyclone Wati intensifies near Suwarrow, with Aitutaki on alert

Tropical Cyclone Wati is strengthening as it moves southeast through the northern Cook Islands, with a cyclone warning in force for Suwarrow atoll and a cyclone alert for Aitutaki in the south.

The Fiji Meteorological Service, which runs the regional warning center for the Cook Islands, rated Wati a Category 1 cyclone on its regional scale at 18:00 GMT on Oct. 16. The storm's center sat near 12.1 S, 163.0 W, about 125 kilometers north of Suwarrow, with a central pressure of 994 hectopascals. The service put maximum sustained winds at about 75 kph, with gusts to 100 kph, and said Wati was moving southeast at about 14 kph. "Cyclone intensifying," the service said in a marine warning that forecast winds near the center to reach 60 knots, about 110 kph, by 18:00 GMT on Oct. 17.

A Tropical Cyclone Warning remains in force for Suwarrow, and a Strong Wind Warning covers the rest of the Northern Cook Islands, according to the service's latest Northern Cooks bulletin, issued at 21:04 GMT. Forecasters expect the center to pass about 50 kilometers east of Suwarrow around 8 p.m. local time Friday and to be about 180 kilometers south-southeast of the atoll by 10 a.m. Saturday. The bulletin warned of damaging gales averaging 65 kph, with gusts to 90 kph, on Suwarrow through Saturday morning, along with "damaging moderate to heavy swells" and a risk of coastal flooding at high tide. It forecast winds of 40 to 55 kph, gusting to 75 kph, elsewhere in the Northern Group.

A Tropical Cyclone Alert remains in force for Aitutaki, the service said in a separate Southern Cooks bulletin issued at 21:00 GMT. It expects Wati's center to be about 530 kilometers north-northwest of Aitutaki by 10 a.m. Saturday and about 100 kilometers north of the island by 10 a.m. Sunday. Damaging gales averaging 75 kph, with gusts to 100 kph, are expected on Aitutaki from early Sunday morning, the bulletin said. A Strong Wind Warning covers the rest of the Southern Group, where winds could rise to 45 kph, gusting to 90 kph, from later Saturday.

Emergency Management Cook Islands urged people, particularly in the Northern Group, to secure loose outdoor items, avoid unnecessary sea travel and follow official updates, Cook Islands News reported.

The U.S. Joint Typhoon Warning Center, which tracks the system as Tropical Cyclone 01P, forecasts a peak of 65 knots, about 120 kph, within 24 to 36 hours, followed by weakening.

The Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System, or GDACS, gave the storm an automated Orange rating. It estimates about 3,815 people could be exposed to strong winds. That figure is a model estimate, not an official count.

The storm arrived early. The official tropical cyclone season begins Nov. 1, Cook Islands News noted.

No damage, casualties or evacuations had been reported as of 21:16 GMT Friday in the sources reviewed for this report. Forecasters plan updated bulletins overnight, and Nexus News Media will update this report.
