# Indonesia battles worst forest fires in over a decade as haze spreads across region

Indonesia is fighting its worst forest and land fires in more than a decade, The Independent reported Friday. Four members of a firefighting team died, and the government has agreed to accept foreign help.

Satellites detected about 799 hotspots nationwide as of Thursday, up from 372 the day before, the forestry ministry said, according to The Independent. Most hotspots were in Sumatra and Borneo.

The four victims belonged to a 12-member team of national park officers, community fire brigade members, volunteers and security personnel. They were fighting a wildfire in Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park in East Java's Probolinggo regency when smoke and flames trapped them, The Independent reported.

"The president has already expressed his approval and the matter is currently being discussed at the technical level within the BNPB," Foreign Minister Arrmanatha Nasir said, according to The Independent. BNPB is Indonesia's disaster mitigation agency. The government has deployed about 60,000 people to fight fires across six provinces, Arrmanatha said, according to CNA. He did not say which countries had offered help or what form it would take, CNA reported.

Indonesia's health ministry has recorded 283,270 cases of acute respiratory infection in fire-affected areas from Aug. 18 to Oct. 13, the Vietnam News Agency reported.

Smoke has also reached neighboring countries. Radio New Zealand reported that haze from fires in Indonesia, including West Papua, has disrupted flights and caused respiratory problems in the region, and is also affecting Papua New Guinea. In Indonesia's Mimika Regency in Central Papua, thick haze from fires in South Papua's Merauke Regency has blanketed the area, RNZ reported, citing Jubi News. Early childhood centers and a primary school there have temporarily closed, and students have switched to remote learning, RNZ reported. RNZ said the fires are concentrated in areas of large-scale land and forest clearance, often for oil palm plantations, and that prolonged dry conditions from an intensifying El Niño have made them worse.

In West Papua, BNPB has run weather modification operations to reduce haze, prioritizing the Fakfak, Teluk Bintuni and Kaimana districts, the state news agency ANTARA reported. ANTARA said the operation was scheduled to end Oct. 15. The meteorological agency BMKG recorded 73 hotspots in West Papua as of Oct. 13, ANTARA said.

Authorities have evacuated at least 46 orangutans from fire-affected areas in West Kalimantan and other parts of Borneo, The Independent reported.

The Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System, or GDACS, has mapped four fires in Indonesia since Sept. 29, each rated "Green," the system's lowest alert level. The largest, in South Sumatra, burned about 11,183 hectares through Oct. 14, and GDACS counted 1,585 people living in its burned area. Three others, in West Papua, South Papua and North Maluku, each burned about 6,000 to 6,500 hectares and ended Oct. 12 or 13. These four alerts represent only a small part of the wider crisis.
