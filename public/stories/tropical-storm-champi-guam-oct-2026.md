# Champi strengthens into a typhoon as Guam braces for a close pass

Champi has strengthened into a typhoon over the western Pacific, and forecasters expect it to pass close to Guam this weekend as a strong typhoon.

The U.S. Joint Typhoon Warning Center upgraded Champi from a tropical storm in its warning 10, issued at 21:00 GMT on Oct. 16. At 18:00 GMT, the storm had maximum sustained winds of 75 knots, about 140 kph, with gusts to 90 knots and a minimum central pressure of 984 millibars. At 21:00 GMT, Champi sat about 328 nautical miles, or 600 kilometers, east-southeast of Andersen Air Force Base, moving west-northwest at 13 knots.

The warning center forecasts 105 knots, about 195 kph, by 18:00 GMT on Oct. 17, with the center near 13.0 N, 145.3 E. It forecasts 120 knots by 06:00 GMT on Oct. 18 and a peak of 135 knots after the storm passes the island. In its forecast discussion, the center said Champi would "continue rapidly intensifying for the next 48 hours" and described its passage "south of the island chain."

The National Weather Service office in Guam has a Typhoon Warning in effect for Guam and Rota, and a Tropical Storm Warning and Typhoon Watch for Tinian and Saipan. Its Flood Watch runs through 4 p.m. local time Sunday, and it said Guam "might get 12 to 20 inches" of rain. The weather service upgraded its Coastal Flood Watch to a Coastal Flood Warning for Guam, Rota, Tinian and Saipan, in effect from 5 p.m. Saturday until 4 a.m. Monday local time. It said coastal inundation of 5 to 7 feet is possible on Guam, adding that a "passage south of Guam would be reduced impacts whereas passage over Guam would result in larger coastal inundation." A High Surf Warning calls for breaking waves up to 35 feet on Guam.

Guam remains in Condition of Readiness 2 under a 90-day state of emergency that Gov. Lou Leon Guerrero declared Friday. The Guam Memorial Hospital Authority has suspended elective and non-emergency services and expects to begin taking in eligible expectant mothers Saturday morning, according to Guam Homeland Security and the Office of Civil Defense. The U.S. Coast Guard set Port Heavy Weather Condition Yankee for the ports of Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands.

The American Red Cross said it will operate or support shelters on all the islands, "with as many as 13 set to open before the storm hits." No shelter openings had been confirmed in the sources reviewed.

The Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System, or GDACS, raised its alert to Red. It estimates 175,254 people could be exposed to Category 1 or stronger winds. That figure is a model estimate, not a count of people affected.

No damage, casualties or evacuation orders had been reported as of 21:40 GMT Friday. The warning center's next warning is due at 03:00 GMT. Nexus News Media will update this report.
