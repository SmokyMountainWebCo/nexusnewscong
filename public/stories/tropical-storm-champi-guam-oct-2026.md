# Tropical Storm Champi heads toward Guam as governor declares emergency

Tropical Storm Champi is moving west across the open Pacific toward Guam, and forecasters expect it to strengthen into a typhoon before it passes over or very near the island.

Gov. Lou Leon Guerrero signed an executive order Friday declaring a state of emergency for Guam ahead of the storm, the Pacific Daily News reported. The order takes effect immediately and lasts 90 days unless extended. The governor placed Guam in Condition of Readiness 2 at 1 p.m. local time Friday, according to Guam Homeland Security and the Office of Civil Defense. Joint Region Marianas declared the same readiness level for its military installations.

At 15:00 GMT on Oct. 16, the storm's center sat near 11.6 N, 150.8 E, about 405 nautical miles, or 750 kilometers, east-southeast of Andersen Air Force Base, according to the U.S. Joint Typhoon Warning Center. At the center's 12:00 GMT analysis, Champi had maximum sustained winds of 60 knots, about 110 kph, with gusts to 75 knots and a minimum central pressure of 992 millibars. It was moving west at 13 knots.

The warning center expects Champi to reach typhoon strength shortly. It forecasts sustained winds of 90 knots by 12:00 GMT on Oct. 17 and 105 knots, about 195 kph, by 00:00 GMT on Oct. 18. The center said the storm would pass "over or very near Guam with maximum sustained winds near 100 kts." It forecasts a peak of 135 knots, about 250 kph, on Oct. 19 as Champi continues west.

The National Weather Service office in Guam has a Typhoon Warning in effect for Guam and Rota, and a Tropical Storm Warning and Typhoon Watch for Tinian and Saipan. A Flood Watch covers all the islands through 4 p.m. local time Oct. 18.

The weather service also issued a High Surf Warning, with surf building as high as 35 feet on Guam, 25 feet on Rota and 20 feet on Tinian and Saipan. A Coastal Flood Watch warns that coastal inundation of 5 to 7 feet is possible on Guam. Both run from 5 p.m. Saturday to 4 a.m. Monday local time.

The Japan Meteorological Agency classifies Champi as a severe tropical storm and expects it to become a typhoon by 03:00 GMT on Oct. 17.

The Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System, or GDACS, has an Orange alert for the storm. It estimates about 80,047 people could be exposed to Category 1 or stronger winds. That figure is a model estimate, not a count of people affected.

No damage, casualties or evacuations had been reported as of Friday in the sources reviewed for this report. The warning center's next warning is due at 21:00 GMT. Nexus News Media will update this report as the storm approaches.
